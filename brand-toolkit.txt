Welcome to the University of Winchester’s Brand
Campaign Toolkit.
This toolkit provides a clear framework to uphold and
amplify Winchester’s distinctive brand campaign identity.
As we navigate the ever-evolving Higher Education
landscape, it ensures our values, voice and visuals are
expressed with clarity and consistency. Inside, you’ll find
the core elements of our campaign — from messaging to
creative visuals. Use it as a shared resource to align our
efforts and create lasting impact.

In The Making
Our campaign concept celebrates how
Winchester helps students bring who
they are into what they do. It speaks
to ‘making’ in both the literal and
figurative sense - not just a metaphor,
but something tangible built into our
curriculum. Within it, we look to foster
a culture of action: trying, testing,
failing forward, and learning by doing.
At Winchester, no one arrives finished.
This is where drafts are welcome.
Where ideas stretch, shift, and sharpen.
Where learning is lived, not just taught.
Small groups. Big questions.
Real work. Real talk.
People who see you – and back you.
We don’t rush to polish.
We stay in the sketch.
We dig into the doing.
This is where the rough cut counts.
Where the mess matters.
You’re not a product at Winchester.
You’re in the making.

Logo Colour
Our logo may be used in Black
or White. Use the elements in
your piece of communication to
determine the colour of your logo
for maximum contrast and clarity.
If your background is light, you
should be using a Black logo. If
your background is dark, you
should be using a White logo.
The logo must only be used in
white or black, never in the core
colour palette.

to maintain this safe area at all times.

Our logo naturally lends itself

Logo Don’ts
The logo should not be misinterpreted,
modified or added to. To the right are
examples of what not to do to the logo.
Don’t add any graphics, symbols or illustrations
to the logo. This includes colours of our brand.
Don’t apply the white logo to a light background.
Don’t stretch, distort or modify the
proportions of the logo.
Don’t alter the arrangement, spacing or structure
of the logo.
Don’t change the colour of the logo.
Don’t add any straplines or secondary
text to the logo.
FACULTY OF EDUCATION AND THE ARTS
Don’t use background imagery where the logo
becomes illegible.

Star Motif
Best in Practice
The star motif is one of the most
frequently used shapes within
the brand, owing to its simplicity,
dynamism, and flexibility across
various assets. It can be applied in
multiple ways, including hero image
applications, texting framing, image
framing and social story frames.
It is effective when placed in
the corners to frame full-bleed
imagery, adding structure without
overwhelming the subject. By allowing
the motif to cut behind elements
such as a student’s head, the framing
creates an integrated look and feel.
Image Framing	It can be used to frame text, adding
clarity and focus to key messaging.
It works especially well when
overlapping the motif to create an
interesting patterning graphic for
CTA frames and video animations.
Text Framing
It is ideal for use on the first frame of a social in-
feed carousel or story video, allowing it to bleed
into the second frame and create continuity.
Here is an example of each way that this motif
can be applied creatively across the asset.
Social Story Frames
It works especially well in dynamic hero image applications, such as social

an eye-catching frame. The organic curves add movement and energy to
large-scale visuals, helping key messaging stand out.
